# GRAVITY'S ARK, the shelf: design ideas compiled and set aside

Status: SHELVED. Nothing here is in any order. Gameplay comes first. Items leave this shelf only by being written into an order and proven there. Every number below is PROPOSED. Every mechanism below is a design choice until an order carries it.

## The three instruments

Nothing in this game speaks on its own. The story is told by prices, by the log, and by the sky. Every narrative item is one of three things: a listing line, a log line, or a thing drawn. Anything that is none of those does not exist in this game. The one exception is dialogue, below, which is lines as data with triggers, and the ship computer, which reads instruments and records aloud.

## Taken

- Her name is the engineer, held in one data field so a later name costs nothing.
- The star is named for death. MORS is proposed: Latin, one syllable, plain on a label, the old catalogue name under Norse-named people. HEL fits the name tables. THANATOS is heavier. The pit keeps the name after the collapse.
- Dialogue is in, as data.
- The ship computer is in, as the ship's own voice.
- The gate's state is rolled by the seed.
- The floppy line is waived for the ark.

## Dialogue as data

- A line is speaker, trigger, condition, text. It fires once, when its trigger fires and its condition holds. It shows on the pane where the event happened and enters the log as a said line under the speaker's name. The card's counts skip said lines, so the naming law is untouched.
- Lines are short, one to two sentences, and each carries one fact. No line repeats. A quiet switch silences every speaker.
- The gate proves every line has a live trigger, every trigger can fire, and no line fires twice. A line about a thing exists only because the thing exists.

The speakers and what each knows:

- The engineer. Terse, speaks in what she needs and what she sees. Her lines double as the help: "Ten seconds. Keep them off me." at the walker; "Three hundred in scrap and I can close that pod." at a repair she cannot start. One line at the collapse. The bill in her words at the gate.
- The hands, by name. One line at hire, one when the hole takes her home world, one when wages go unpaid. The dead do not speak.
- The station clerk, one voice per faction. The Charter sells the rumour and talks fuel and the star. The Authority talks the toll and the gate. The Militia talks holding ground. The Fitters speak in prices with one flat line. The Wreckers speak only in the demand.
- The ship, below.
- The card, in the ending's line.

The backstory these lines carry, one fact per line, never twice: MORS is dying and everyone has known for years. The gate at the far end is shut, or held, or dear. Only an engineer of her kind can open a broken gate, and she is the last one on this side. Each faction wants her for its own reason. The Fitters need her because she keeps them running. The Militia believes worlds can be held. The tramps leaving are the people who could pay. What is past the gate, nobody says. The Grip stays unexplained.

## The ship computer

- The ship speaking. Lines in the same table, speaker SHIP. Triggers are the ship's own events and thresholds: launch, dock, a listing moving past a band, a star stage, the collapse, the schedule, the hull's edge moving, fuel under the return line, wages due, a lock, a shot, the bill, the toll.
- Flat, numeric, complete sentences. Never advice, never a feeling. "Light from MORS ceased 2.3 seconds ago." "Fuel at this step is up forty percent since the last dock." "The edge reaches this world in 112 seconds."
- It alone names the swelling. The people never do. "MORS is 14 percent larger than at launch."
- It reads its records where people only rumour: "Gate record: shut three years, forty-one days. Last opened for an Authority hull." The record is stale by a seeded number of years, so the ship can be wrong.
- The registry of worlds is its memory: visits, parts left, infection, loss. The Grip it reports and never explains: "Contaminant aboard. Scrub available at any station."
- The duet: the ship states, the engineer answers in what she needs. "The edge reaches this world in ninety seconds." "Then the pod gets welded or it stays."
- Unnamed. At the end the card is the ship's own record, signed with the name the galaxy gave you.
- One line per trigger, once. Nothing the panes already show in numbers is repeated in words. The ship speaks at thresholds, not continuously.

## The gate by seed

Data on the galaxy: a state, a holder, a toll, and a bill when broken.

- Working, high toll. No fix. The toll rolls from a band two to four times the normal one. The seed pulls toward sold her and passed.
- Broken. Her bill in time, scrap, and modules, then the toll.
- Blockaded. The gate works, but a faction holds it instead of the Authority. Its ships sit there under the Wreckers' lock law. Passing means meeting the holder's demand or running it under fire into the gate. Each holder demands in its own coin: the Militia takes hands, the Wreckers take her or the cargo, the Fitters take modules, the Charter takes credits over the toll. The toll goes to whoever holds the gate.
- Broken and blockaded. Both.

Weights, PROPOSED: working with a high toll 25, broken 40, blockaded 25, both 10. The record's staleness band: one to five years. The holder's demand size: the Wreckers' law for her or cargo; one hand per crew of four for the Militia; one module per three welded for the Fitters; the toll again for the Charter.

The map shows the gate from the start, as ruled. Its state is the one thing the map does not show, PROPOSED: the ship's record holds the last known state, the Charter's rumour sells the current one and the bill, arrival shows the truth. Running a blockade is allowed under fire, PROPOSED. Fighting through waits on the hull's guns, below.

How it lands: the galaxy module rolls state and holder with the gate. The gate module's pass rule reads them: fixed or not broken, toll paid to the holder, blockade met or run. The price module's roost, sight, lock, and demand become the blockade with the holder's coin swapped in. The log gets its lines: passed the Militia's blockade, ran the Wreckers' guns. The endings stay five.

## The engineer

- Her time is her want. She fights or she fixes, never both. No second want.
- Her price rises every ring and every faction lists it. The hire-out pays and stops her repairs. The sale prints the card where it happens. Her death ends the delivery and the crossing goes on.
- Her rescue on foot, from the slice: the walker down under her puts her on the ground. She walks home at her own pace unless the stick is taken. Boarding needs four metres. The Grip claw a person at 12 hp/s.

## The hands

- Names from the seed, women and men.
- A hand unpaid at a dock walks: the seed picks who, the hand joins that station's pool, the roster shows the gap.
- A hand's home is the station that hired her. When the hole takes it, the log says so by name.
- The dead die by name in the log, and the card lists who did not come through, by name and where.

## The factions, in prices

- The Authority holds the gate when nobody else does, sets the toll, pays bounties.
- The Charter holds the markets, hires her out, and sells the rumour: a listing line that shows the gate's state and bill before you reach it, for a price.
- The Militia conscripts: one hand per dock at its stations unless a levy is paid, PROPOSED. Its walker is the boss on the crash world. A front is where it tries again.
- The Fitters are machines, PROPOSED. They buy scrap, spares, modules, and her, never people. The button that sells people at the gate goes.
- The Wreckers demand her or the cargo, whichever lists higher.

## The Grip

- Never explained.
- Its one story mechanism, from the slice, PROPOSED in: a clawed hull carries the Grip, a landing marks the world, the dead rise there at 3.5 s, a station scrubs the hull for a price. Your own road spreads it.
- The first line in the log is the walker's driver's name, and he walks in the first wave.

## The leaving

- Tramps, PROPOSED for a later order: plain bodies on a burn schedule leaving the near stations for the gate after the first landing and never coming back. The sky's only other traffic. Wrecks after the shell.

## The storyteller

- Its own module, later. A quiet dial at zero that reproduces today's clocks exactly, proven by twin identity, so the measured wave baseline survives. Then rules as data: it may delay a wave, never shrinks one, never touches the hole, and times the hire-out and the people contract by need.

## Places

- Roles, never names. The ash world under the Militia. The Charter's step. The Wreckers' roost. Role words for climates, holders, and stations are a table.

## The card

- The manifest. Who came through, by name. Who did not, by name and where. What the hole ate. The galaxy's three-word name from what was done. The ship signs it.

## Presentation that carries story

- The star in every frame, bigger and redder per landing. The held shot at the collapse: the camera goes to MORS and stays a few seconds. The ground's light reddens per landing and the shadows lengthen. A found module wears the station it came from.

## Holes noted

- The module table sells mounts, racks, shields, thrusters, and the mech bay with no mechanism behind them. Nothing on the page sells them yet. Until their mechanism lands they are withheld from every listing, PROPOSED. The demo carries the slug driver, the missiles, and the shield.
- The build screen has not reached the page.
- The mech bay as a mechanism: the walker rides only if a mech bay is welded.
- The visitor model: named in the design document, undefined.
